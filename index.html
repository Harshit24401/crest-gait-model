Can a single camera tell you which crutch is better?

CREST's vision gait model estimates gait quality from ordinary sagittal-view video — no markers, no force plates — by extracting biomechanical keypoints with MediaPipe Pose and scoring them against reference gait patterns from open clinical datasets.

2. Dataset & calibration

No public dataset compares different crutch designs on the same subjects — that comparison is this capstone's own contribution. These datasets calibrate the scoring system: what does "healthy" vs. "impaired/assisted" gait actually look like, numerically, from real video.

GAVD

Gait Abnormality in Video Dataset

Curated annotations over public sagittal-view walking videos, labeled by clinical gait pattern (normal, antalgic, stroke, prosthetic, myopathic, cerebral palsy, and more).

  • 54 sagittal clips used (19 healthy, 35 impaired/assisted)
  • Trimmed to each clip's annotated gait segment before processing
  • Held-out split: 40 training / 11 validation clips

GaitRec

Large-scale ground reaction force dataset

Published clinical population statistics (demographics, impairment class) used as an independent numeric cross-check on what "impaired gait" looks like in a real clinical cohort.

  • Metadata only — no force-plate signal our vision pipeline can consume directly
  • Used to sanity-check population framing, not as pipeline input

3. How a video becomes a score

  1. TrimCut to the annotated gait segment — avoids wasting inference on irrelevant footage
  2. Pose extractionMediaPipe Pose (CPU delegate) — 16 tracked landmarks per frame: shoulders, elbows, wrists, hips, knees, ankles, heels, toes
  3. Gait eventsHeel-strike from peaks in heel height; toe-off from a sustained drop in toe velocity
  4. Per-stride metricsStride time, cadence, stance ratio, peak knee flexion, trunk lean, elbow ROM — all 2D geometry, no fabricated depth
  5. Reference bandsPooled healthy vs. impaired median & IQR per metric, built from GAVD
  6. Scoring0 = matches impaired-band median, 1 = matches healthy-band median — a position on the impaired-to-healthy spectrum, not a pass/fail cutoff

6. Justification & limitations

Why median/IQR, not mean/SD?

Gait metrics from real-world video are skewed and outlier-prone. Median and IQR are robust to that in a way mean/SD aren't — a few noisy strides don't drag the whole reference band around.

Why exclude some metrics from the verdict?

Trunk lean showed almost no separation between healthy and impaired medians (3.8° vs 4.0°). Scoring against a near-zero gap amplifies noise into meaningless swings — so it's reported, but excluded from the composite score. Only metrics with real separation count toward the decision.

Single sagittal camera

No left-right symmetry, no lateral trunk sway, no crutch-plant kinematics from vision — those need a second camera or are reserved for the crutch's own IMU. This model reports what's actually observable from one side-on view.

2D only

MediaPipe's monocular depth (z) is a rough relative estimate, not a real measurement. Every angle here is computed in image-plane (x, y) only — no fabricated 3D precision.
